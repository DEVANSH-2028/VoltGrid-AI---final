# VoltGrid AI — Current Project Specification

## Document purpose

This document describes the current VoltGrid AI prototype in this repository. It is an as-built product and behavior specification, not a claim that every possible enterprise capability or integration is production-ready. Future enhancements are identified separately at the end.

## 1. Product identity and goals

**Product:** VoltGrid AI — a geopolitical- and tariff-resilient sourcing network for EV battery assembly.

**Positioning:** A SAP Side-by-Side AI Extension for SAP BTP that extends SAP S/4HANA, SAP Ariba, SAP IBP, and SAP Build Process Automation. VoltGrid is not a replacement ERP.

**Business context:** The prototype represents an LFP battery assembly plant in Chakan, Pune, with an illustrative annual procurement value of ₹180 Cr and a network of 14 suppliers. Its business goals are 99.2% plant uptime, more than 50% domestic value addition (DVA), and compressing disruption response from 72 hours to minutes.

All business values and seeded records are demonstration data unless a live source is explicitly identified. The goals above are not measured production guarantees.

## 2. Active persona and authority

The application has one active persona: **Meera Nair**, EV Sourcing Planner · Chakan Plant, Pune (`planner@voltgrid.ai`).

- The frontend opens directly as Meera; there is no user-facing login screen or role switcher.
- The backend seeds demo user records and provides authentication endpoints for the app's automatic session.
- Meera has end-to-end demo authority, including approval/rejection/revision of escalations, SAP sandbox synchronization, audit-log access, and report export.
- Do not add additional selectable personas or expose manager/auditor roles in the product UI without an explicit product decision.

## 3. Product experience: eight active modules

The persistent navigation contains exactly these eight modules. Older URLs redirect to a current module; they do not represent additional active modules.

### 3.1 Executive Control Tower

Shows the Chakan operating overview, including procurement value, supplier count, weighted risk, DVA, inventory coverage, at-risk purchase orders and financial exposure. It also shows spend by country, sourcing composition, critical alerts, recent incident activity, and integration status. The crisis-playbook action starts a disruption simulation.

### 3.2 Digital Twin & Suppliers

Displays the Chakan plant and supplier locations on a Leaflet/OpenStreetMap map, plus supplier risk, quality, reliability, lead-time, origin, and DVA-eligibility details. Selecting a supplier on the map or in the directory synchronizes the selected record. Port locations are illustrative.

### 3.3 Disruption & Split-Order Lab

Provides scenario controls for tariff increase, delay, currency depreciation, supplier-capacity reduction, inventory, and demand. Its tabs support scenario execution, document upload/verification, and comparison of balanced, cost, domestic-heavy, and lowest-risk strategies. Scenarios produce proposed supplier allocations for review; they do not automatically change live SAP data.

### 3.4 AI Agent Operations

Shows the three backend services—VoltSense, VoltRoute, and VoltGuard—and the incident execution history. A run records agent outputs and displays their sequence. These are deterministic application services and rules, not independent autonomous LLM agents.

### 3.5 Risk & PLI Compliance

Shows the latest scenario risk assessment, its six weighted factors, an illustrative DVA gauge, and eligible supplier records. The factors are tariff exposure (25%), inventory/stockout (25%), supplier concentration (15%), geopolitical exposure (15%), logistics delay (10%), and PLI/DVA exposure (10%). A provider explanation can use SAP Generative AI Hub when configured; rule-based, database-grounded answers are available with the fallback provider.

The demonstration DVA bands are SAFE above 52%, WARNING from 50% through 52%, and CRITICAL below 50%. They are configurable prototype assumptions, not legal advice or an authoritative PLI calculation.

### 3.6 Approval Center (HITL)

Lists scenario approvals with before/after sourcing context, financial delta, DVA impact, risk indicators, and the VoltGuard result. Meera can approve, reject, or request a revision. Approval persists in the application database (Neon in deployment) and initiates the configured SAP synchronization workflow; rejection and revision requests are recorded without creating an approved reallocation.

### 3.7 SAP Extension & Audit

Shows live-versus-fallback SAP connection status, permits a connection test, exposes the latest generated approval payload, and displays the governance audit log. It provides audit CSV export and a PDF summary report.

### 3.8 Sustainability Intelligence

Shows illustrative emissions and sourcing information with source and methodology labels. Values are not verified carbon accounting.

## 4. Disruption and decision workflow

The current simulator runs this backend workflow:

1. Create a disruption event and incident from user-selected scenario inputs.
2. VoltSense reads supplier and inventory data through the SAP gateway, identifies affected suppliers/components, and labels the scenario source as unverified simulation input.
3. The deterministic risk engine calculates a weighted risk score and stores its factor breakdown.
4. VoltRoute ranks available suppliers and proposes a split allocation using a weighted heuristic over landed-cost estimate, quality, reliability, lead time, concentration, domestic sourcing, and the selected strategy.
5. VoltGuard evaluates the proposed DVA against the 50% target and 52% safe buffer.
6. The system records agent executions, risk and compliance assessments, allocations, incident status, and an audit event. Commercial or compliance conditions can create a pending human approval.
7. Meera's approval creates local purchase-order records and attempts SAP synchronization when configured. SAP sync results and the generated payload are recorded in the audit trail.

The allocation logic is a deterministic prototype heuristic, not a mathematical solver or a production purchasing recommendation. Identical inputs and seed data should produce repeatable outcomes.

## 5. Integrations and data-source behavior

### SAP Side-by-Side gateway

The backend has adapters for SAP S/4HANA, Ariba, IBP, and Build Process Automation.

- `SAP_MODE="auto"` uses a configured live endpoint where available and otherwise uses seeded local records.
- `SAP_MODE="fallback"` selects Local Sandbox Only.
- The SAP Data Routing settings control the in-memory mode for the running backend.
- Connection status distinguishes live SAP responses from local fallback. A connection is not labelled live unless a live request succeeds.
- SAP request timeouts are bounded by `SAP_TIMEOUT_MS` and capped at 2500 ms.
- S/4HANA may use an API Hub key or bearer token. Ariba and IBP use configured endpoint URLs and bearer tokens. Build Process Automation uses a webhook URL and bearer token; the connection test does not invoke the webhook.
- Live purchase-order writes are disabled by default. Enabling them requires `SAP_ALLOW_LIVE_WRITE="true"` and configured company code, purchasing organization, purchasing group, and plant mappings.
- Local approval persistence is retained if a live SAP synchronization fails. Demo supplier and plant codes are not guaranteed to exist in a connected tenant.

### Other external data

- Currency conversion requests Frankfurter on demand and returns a deterministic estimate when the request is unavailable.
- The news and weather endpoints currently return illustrative demo signals; they are not live GDELT or Open-Meteo ingestion.
- The supplier map uses OpenStreetMap tiles. Supplier, plant, and port coordinates remain illustrative seed data.
- External signals, estimates, and user-entered disruption scenarios require verification before operational use.

### AI explanations

The backend supports SAP Generative AI Hub through SAP's orchestration SDK. Configure `LLM_PROVIDER="sap-genai-hub"`, `SAP_GENAI_MODEL`, and an AI Core service key in `backend\.env`. The deterministic `LLM_PROVIDER="fallback"` option does not require an external model. Keep all service keys on the backend and out of source control. Provider configuration or request failures are returned as errors rather than presented as successful generated answers.

## 6. Data model and persistence

The deployed application uses Prisma with PostgreSQL on Neon. `DATABASE_URL` is the pooled application connection and `DATABASE_URL_UNPOOLED` is the direct migration connection. The existing SQLite database is retained as a read-only, one-time import source. The schema contains:

- Business/reference data: User, Plant, Supplier, Component, BOM, Inventory, and PurchaseOrder/PurchaseOrderItem.
- Disruption and decisions: DisruptionEvent, Incident, AgentExecution, RiskAssessment, SupplierAllocation, ComplianceAssessment, and Approval.
- Evidence and operations: AuditLog, UploadedDocument, ExternalDataSource, SimulationScenario, and SustainabilityRecord.

The destructive demo seed creates one Chakan plant, 14 baseline suppliers, six components, inventory, six purchase orders, illustrative disruptions, integration-source records, and demo users. The migrated SQLite dataset contains 43 suppliers, 19 components, and existing incident, approval, FX snapshot, and audit history.

**Data safety:** Preserve existing PostgreSQL data. Prisma migrations are versioned under `backend/prisma/migrations`; do not use `db push`, drop tables, or reseed a database containing data that must be kept. `npm run db:import:sqlite -- --confirm-empty-target` copies the local SQLite records only when every VoltGrid model table in PostgreSQL is empty. The SQLite file is opened read-only. `npm run db:seed:demo` is destructive and must only be used on a disposable database.

## 7. Technology and repository structure

- Frontend: React 19, Vite, JavaScript, React Router, custom CSS, Recharts, Leaflet, and Lucide.
- Backend: Node.js, Express, Socket.IO, and Prisma.
- Persistence: Neon PostgreSQL in deployment; SQLite retained only as a migration source.
- Integrations: SAP AI SDK for orchestration, SAP extension adapters, PDFKit for PDF output, Multer for uploads, and PapaParse in the backend dependencies.
- Workspace scripts are defined in the root, `frontend`, and `backend` package manifests.

Main implementation locations:


The design uses a crisp SAP Fiori / industrial enterprise palette: navy `#142C43`, blue `#1769AA`, electric blue `#16A6D9`, green `#26966B`, amber `#E39A28`, red `#C94B42`, canvas `#F4F7FA`, white `#FFFFFF`, and text `#1C2A38`. Avoid dark neon AI styling.

## 8. Local development

From the project root:

```sh
npm ci
npm run prisma:generate
npm run db:migrate:deploy
npm run dev
```

The root `.env.local` is loaded by backend and migration commands and is git-ignored. Use a pooled Neon URL for `DATABASE_URL` and a direct URL for `DATABASE_URL_UNPOOLED`. In development, Vite proxies API and Socket.IO requests to Express; in production, Express serves the built React app and API from one origin.

Useful checks:

```powershell
npm run build
npm test
```

The root `npm test` command runs the backend's Node.js test suite. Tests currently cover risk calculations, allocation optimization, SAP extension behavior, and SAP Generative AI Hub behavior. The frontend build is provided by Vite.

## 9. Current implementation boundaries

The following limitations are part of the current baseline and must not be described as completed production features:

- The eight-module navigation consolidates former standalone areas. Legacy paths redirect to current modules; there is no standalone procurement, reports, or settings module.
- Sustainability values are illustrative and are not verified carbon accounting.
- Uploaded PDF/CSV files are stored with basic metadata and a verification flag. The current upload path does not perform reliable PDF/CSV text extraction or policy interpretation.
- News and weather responses are demo signals, not live event feeds. Currency conversion may be live, with a deterministic fallback.
- Supplier allocation is a weighted heuristic, not a certified optimization solver.
- PDF output is a generic illustrative summary. The audit log has CSV export; a complete library of detailed report types is not implemented.
- The simulation reset endpoint records a reset request but retains seeded ERP and incident data.
- DVA/PLI, risk exposure, carbon values, supplier coordinates, and other seeded data are illustrative and require independent validation.
- A live SAP tenant is not required for local use. Without configured credentials/endpoints, SAP screens use local fallback data.

Never claim a live SAP connection, verified policy event, legal compliance result, audited carbon measurement, or production-ready sourcing recommendation unless the relevant data source and validation have actually been established.

## 10. Current acceptance baseline

The implementation should continue to satisfy these repository-level outcomes:

- The local app starts and exposes the seven defined navigation modules with Meera as the only active persona.
- The seeded local experience works without SAP or AI-provider credentials.
- A disruption scenario records the sequential agent outputs, risk breakdown, DVA assessment, allocation, incident status, and audit history.
- Pending human escalations can be approved, rejected, or returned for revision by Meera.
- SAP reads use live data only when configured and successful; otherwise the UI identifies the fallback source.
- SAP purchase-order writes remain opt-in and require explicit master-data configuration.
- Build and backend tests pass, and errors are surfaced rather than shown as successful integration results.
